Germany guide

Everything you need, before and after you land

Please check before you act. The main figures here — blocked account, BAföG, minimum wage, Blue Card salary thresholds, Deutschlandticket, student health insurance and integration-course cost — were checked against public sources in October 2026. Other details come from our own experience and may be out of date, and rules change often. This is not legal, immigration or financial advice: confirm current requirements with the official sources listed under the Visas tab.

🚌

Semesterticket

Most German universities include a Semesterticket — free or heavily discounted local and regional public transport, usually valid across the whole state. Many also offer the Deutschland-Semesterticket upgrade (about €37.80 a month in 2026) for regional travel nationwide.

Value: often hundreds of euros per semester (varies by university)

💶

BAföG — Student Financial Aid

Government student aid of up to €992/month in 2026. Half is a grant, half an interest-free loan, and total repayment is capped at €10,010. EU and some non-EU students qualify after a period of study.

Apply via: bafög-digital.de

🍽️

Mensa & Studentenwerk

Student canteens (Mensa) run by Studentenwerk serve hot meals for roughly €2–5 with a student ID, depending on the city. Also subsidised housing, psychological counselling, and child care.

Typical lunch: about €2–5 with student card

💊

Student Health Insurance

Statutory health insurance for students costs roughly €140–150/month in 2026 (for example €141–146 at TK, including long-term care insurance). It is free if you are covered under your parents' German insurance (Familienversicherung), generally until 25.

Compare funds — prices differ by only a few euros

🧑‍💻

Werkstudent Jobs

During the semester you can generally work up to 20 hours/week as a Werkstudent with reduced social-security contributions. Non-EU students are also limited to 140 full days or 280 half days a year. Typical pay is €15–20/hour.

Find on: XING, LinkedIn, university job boards

🏦

Blocked Account (Sperrkonto)

To get your student visa you generally deposit €11,904 (€992 × 12) in a blocked account for 2026. It is released at €992 a month. Fintiba and Expatrio are the main providers, with setup in a few days online.

Check the current amount on the Federal Foreign Office site

📋

Recognition of Qualifications

Germany officially recognises foreign degrees. Use anabin.kmk.org to check your degree's status. For regulated professions, apply via the relevant state authority.

Check: anabin.kmk.org or anerkennung-in-deutschland.de

🔍

Job Portals for Germany

LinkedIn and XING are top networks. StepStone, Indeed.de, and make-it-in-germany.com (government portal for international workers) for listings.

Best: LinkedIn + StepStone + company websites

📄

German CV Format

German CVs (Lebenslauf) often include a professional photo, date of birth, and nationality. Keep it to 2 pages. Include an Anschreiben (cover letter) and copies of certificates.

Use Europass or Canva for a clean template

🗣️

German Language

B1–B2 German opens most doors. Goethe Institut, Volkshochschule (VHS) courses and DW Deutsch are great starting points — VHS courses are usually much cheaper than private schools.

Cheap option: VHS courses (prices vary by city)

💰

Minimum Wage & Rights

The statutory minimum wage is €13.90/hour in 2026 and rises to €14.60 on 1 January 2027. All workers regardless of visa status are entitled to paid holidays (minimum 20 days/year), sick pay, and maternity/paternity leave.

Minimum wage: €13.90/hour (2026)

🧾

Arbeitsagentur Support

The Federal Employment Agency offers free job counselling and, for eligible people, funding for job-related German courses and further training. Register at arbeitsagentur.de.

Ask about funded German and training options

Most common

Student Visa · §16b AufenthG

Studienvisum — Student Visa

For anyone admitted to a German university (Uni or Hochschule). You'll need an admission letter, proof of funds via a blocked account (Sperrkonto), health insurance, and a valid passport. Valid for duration of studies + 18 months job search after graduation.

💰 Blocked account: €11,904/year (€992/month) in 2026
⏱ Processing: Varies — often 4–12 weeks at the German embassy
💼 Work allowed: 140 full days or 280 half days/year

Job Seeker Visa · §20 AufenthG

Arbeitsuchende — Job Seeker Visa

6-month visa to come to Germany and search for a job. You must have a recognised foreign degree, sufficient German language skills (usually B1–B2), and enough savings for the stay. You cannot work during this period — only interview. Many job seekers now apply for the newer Opportunity Card instead.

💰 Funds required: Roughly €1,000+ per month — confirm with the embassy
⏱ Duration: 6 months, non-renewable
📋 Key: Degree must be recognised in Germany

Skilled Worker · §18 AufenthG

Fachkräftevisum — Skilled Worker Visa

For professionals with a recognised vocational qualification or degree and a concrete job offer from a German employer. Since the 2023 Skilled Immigration Act, qualifications from non-EU countries are more easily recognised.

📋 Required: Job offer + recognised qualification
⏱ Processing: Varies by embassy + employer
🔄 Leads to: Permanent residence after 4 years

EU Blue Card · §18g AufenthG

Blaue Karte EU — EU Blue Card

For highly qualified workers with a university degree and a job offer above the salary threshold (€50,700/year in 2026; €45,934.20 for shortage occupations, certain IT specialists and recent graduates). Faster route to permanent residence.

💰 Min salary: €50,700/yr (or €45,934.20 for shortage jobs, IT, recent graduates)
🏠 PR after: 21 months with B1 German, 27 months otherwise
👨‍👩‍👧 Family: Spouse gets immediate work authorisation

Opportunity Card · §20a AufenthG

Chancenkarte — Opportunity Card

Available since 1 June 2024. A points-based visa to come to Germany and look for work — even without a confirmed job offer. Points are awarded for qualifications, language skills, work experience, age, and previous ties to Germany. You need basic German (A1) or English (B2) and proof you can support yourself, and you can work part-time (up to 20h/week) while searching.

⭐ Points needed: 6 points from the criteria catalogue
💼 Part-time: Up to 20h/week while on this card
⏱ Duration: Up to 1 year; a follow-up card or work permit is possible with a qualifying job offer

Family Reunion · §28–30 AufenthG

Familiennachzug — Family Reunion

Join a spouse, parent or child already living legally in Germany. Spouses generally need basic German (A1 level) before the visa is issued. Children under 16 can usually join without language proof. Immediate work authorisation granted.

🗣 German needed: A1 for spouses (Goethe A1 certificate)
💼 Work rights: Immediate work authorisation granted
📋 Sponsor needs: Valid residence permit + adequate housing

01

Anmeldung — City Registration

Register your address at the local Bürgeramt within 14 days. You'll need your passport, rental agreement, and a filled Anmeldeformular. You'll receive a Meldebescheinigung — the key document for everything else.

→ Book your slot online — walk-in queues are long

02

Bank Account

Open a German bank account as soon as you have your Anmeldung. Popular choices for newcomers: N26 (online, English), DKB (online) or Commerzbank (branches everywhere). Check current fees and account conditions before you choose.

→ Many online banks let you open the account entirely online

03

Health Insurance

Health insurance is mandatory. As a student apply through a statutory fund such as TK, Barmer or AOK (roughly €140–150/month in 2026). As an employee your employer registers you with the fund you choose — pick your Krankenkasse before starting.

→ Statutory funds cost almost the same — compare services and the Zusatzbeitrag

04

SIM Card

Get a German SIM immediately — you need a local number for almost all registrations. Popular prepaid brands: Aldi Talk (O2 network), Lidl Connect (Vodafone network) and congstar (Telekom network). No contract needed; plans usually run in 4-week packs.

→ Starter sets cost around €10 — compare coverage where you live

05

Steuer-ID (Tax ID)

Your 11-digit Steueridentifikationsnummer arrives by post 2–4 weeks after Anmeldung — automatic, no application needed. Give it to your employer so tax is calculated correctly.

→ Keep this number safe — it's yours for life in Germany

06

Finding Housing

WG-Gesucht.de for flatshares (WGs). ImmobilienScout24 and Immowelt for full apartments. Kleinanzeigen (formerly eBay Kleinanzeigen) for private listings. Studentenwerk housing for students (waitlists exist). Never pay before viewing.

→ Start WG-Gesucht searches 6–8 weeks before arrival

07

Deutschlandticket

€63/month in 2026 (rising to €66.80 from January 2027) for unlimited travel on regional trains, S-Bahns, U-Bahns, trams and buses across Germany — not ICE/IC. It is a monthly-cancellable subscription and great value if you travel regularly.

→ Buy via the DB Navigator app, bahn.de or your local transport provider

08

Integration Course

Government-subsidised German language + orientation course — 700 lessons in total, ending in a B1 exam. Cost: €2.29 per lesson (€1,603 in total); half is refunded if you pass the final test in time, and some benefit recipients are exempt. Check availability and eligibility with BAMF (bamf.de).

→ Apply for a cost exemption before the course starts
